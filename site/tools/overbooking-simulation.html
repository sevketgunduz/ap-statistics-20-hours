Overbooking Simulation

A St Mary's clinic session has 10 appointment slots. Each booked patient misses the appointment with the probability you choose, independently of the others. One trial is one whole clinic session: every booked patient gets a random digit, and the digit decides whether they turn up. Run trials, choose the event you care about, and watch its relative frequency as the number of trials grows.

Try this first

The tool opens with a run of 20 trials, the same size as the hand simulation. Press Run 10 trials a few times and watch the coloured line jump. Then press Run 1,000 trials several times. The line settles: that is the law of large numbers. Its relative frequency after many trials is the estimate of the probability.

Then the trap

Press Start a new run and run 20 trials again. The new run's estimate after 20 trials is rarely the same as the first run's. After 5,000 trials the runs agree to about two decimal places. A handful of trials gives an estimate; it does not give the probability.

And the decision

Switch between 10, 11 and 12 booked, and between the events. Booking more patients makes an unused slot less likely and an overfull session more likely. The simulation cannot choose for St Mary's; it tells the manager how often each thing will happen.
