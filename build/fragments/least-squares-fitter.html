Least-squares Fitter

Drag the two orange handles, or focus one with Tab and use the arrow keys, to move your line. Every residual, and the sum of their squares, updates as you go, and so does the residual plot underneath. The least-squares line is the one line that makes that sum as small as it can be. Try to find it before you ask to see it.

Try this first

On Lincoln High, turn on squares of the residuals and move the handles until Σ(residual²) is as small as you can make it. Write your best value down. Then press Show the least-squares line. Nobody beats it: the least-squares line is defined as the line with the smallest possible sum. Notice how much of the total one square contributes: the student at 2.5 km who took 33 minutes.

Then this

Press Flat line at ȳ. That line predicts the mean for everyone and ignores x completely. Its Σ(residual²) is far bigger. Tilt it and watch the sum fall. Then try Three points: the least-squares line is ŷ = 1 + 0.5x with Σ(residual²) = 1.5, and the lines y = x and y = 2 both give 2.

And the catch

Switch to the café's iced drinks, show the least-squares line and move your line onto it. It is the best straight line there is, r² = 0.92, and its residual plot still curves: positive at both ends, negative in the middle. Least squares finds the best line; it does not tell you whether a line is the right model. The residual plot does.

Beyond the course description

Why r² is a proportion: for Lincoln High the flat line at ȳ leaves Σ(residual²) = 4098.2 and the least-squares line leaves 438.3, and 1 − 438.3 ÷ 4098.2 = 0.893 = r². The course description states what r² means, not this calculation, so it is not examinable.
